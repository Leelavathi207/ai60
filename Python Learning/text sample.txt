Google Search works in three core steps: crawling the web with automated bots, indexing and storing page data in a massive database, and ranking/serving the most relevant results when a user types a query.You can read a detailed breakdown in the official In-Depth Guide to How Google Search Works.CrawlingAutomated software called Googlebot (or crawlers/spiders) explores the internet.Bots find new or updated web pages by following links from existing pages.They download text, images, and videos from these sites.IndexingGoogle processes and analyzes the downloaded pages.It reads text, looks at images or video files, and notes key content.All of this analyzed data is stored in the Google index, a massive digital database.Ranking and Serving ResultsWhen you search, Google looks through its index for matching pages.Algorithms sort the matches based on hundreds of clues, including:Relevance to your specific words.Content quality and usefulness.Your location and language.The device you use (phone or computer).The top results appear on your screen in a fraction of a second.Would you like to know more about how ranking factors work or how to get a website indexed by Google?
